DIRECT EXAMINATION BY MS. CHEN:
 
Q Please state your name for the record.
 
A Marcus P. Pig. People call me Pig One.
 
Q Mr. Pig, where did you reside in the spring
of this year?
 
A I had a home. On Meadowbrook Lane.
 
Q Can you describe the construction of that home?
 
A It was a straw house. I built it myself.
 
Q How long did it take to build?
 
A About a week. Maybe less.
 
MR. BRIGGS: Objection. Relevance, Your Honor.
 
THE COURT: Overruled. Continue, Ms. Chen.
 
Q On the morning of March 14th, did something
happen to your home?
 
A Yes. The wolf came.
 
Q The defendant? B.B. Wolf, seated at the
defense table?
 
A Yes, ma’am.
 
Q What did the defendant do when he arrived?
 
A He stood outside. He threatened me. Said
he would blow the house down.
 
Q In those words?
 
A He said he’d huff and he’d puff and he’d blow
the house in. Yes.
 
Q And did he carry out that threat?
 
A He huffed. And he puffed. And yes. The house
came down.
 
Q And where did you go after your house fell?
 
A I ran to my brother’s house.
 
Q The stick house?
 
A Yes, ma’am.
 
MS. CHEN: No further questions at this time,
Your Honor.

CROSS-EXAMINATION BY MR. BRIGGS:
 
Q Mr. Pig, you testified that you built this
home yourself?
 
A That’s right.
 
Q Are you a licensed contractor?
 
A I’m — no. I’m not.
 
Q Did you pull a building permit for this
structure?
 
A I didn’t know I needed one.
 
Q Isn’t it true, Mr. Pig, that straw is not
a recognized structural building material
under Northern Plains Code Section 142.7?
 
A I — I didn’t know that at the time.
 
THE COURT: Mr. Briggs. The Northern Plains Building
Code is not at issue in this proceeding.
Move on.
 
MR. BRIGGS: Yes, Your Honor.
 
Q Mr. Pig, the wind that morning was recorded
at eighteen miles per hour. Is that correct?
 
A I don’t know what the wind was.
 
Q Could natural wind have caused a straw
structure to fail?
 
A It wasn’t the wind.
 
Q How can you be certain?
 
A Because I saw him. I looked right through the
window. He was standing there, blowing. I
could hear him.
 
Q You were frightened?
 
A Terrified.
 
Q And fear can distort perception, can it not?
 
A I know what I saw.
 
Q You were alone in the house?
 
A Yes.
 
Q No corroborating witnesses?
 
A I was alone. Yes. Because I had just built
the house and moved in. Two days prior.
 
MR. BRIGGS: No further questions.

(Witness Theodore G. Pig, previously sworn, takes
the stand.)
 
DIRECT EXAMINATION BY MS. CHEN:
 
Q Theodore — you prefer Ted?
 
A Ted is fine.
 
Q Ted, what is your relationship to the previous
witness?
 
A Marcus is my brother. Pig One.
 
Q On the afternoon of March 14th, what happened?
 
A Marcus came running to my house. He was out
of breath. Couldn’t even speak for a moment.
He told me the wolf destroyed his house.
 
Q What did you do?
 
A I let him in. I told him he was safe.
 
Q Were you confident your home could withstand
a similar attack?
 
A I built with sticks. Hardwood. Good material.
I thought so, yes.
 
Q Did the defendant come to your house?
 
A Within the hour. He came to the door.
 
Q What did he say?
 
A Same thing. He said he’d huff and he’d puff
and he’d blow our house in.
 
MR. BRIGGS: Objection. Hearsay.
 
THE COURT: It’s a verbal act, Mr. Briggs. Not offered
for the truth of the matter. Overruled.
 
Q And did he follow through?
 
A He did. It took him longer this time. But the
sticks gave way. Both of us inside. The house
just — collapsed. Around us.
 
Q And then what happened?
 
A We ran. As fast as we could. To our sister’s
house.
 
Q Your sister — Priscilla?
 
A Yes. She has the brick house. We knew if
anywhere was going to hold, it was hers.
 
MS. CHEN: No further questions.

(Witness Priscilla M. Pig, sworn.)
 
DIRECT EXAMINATION BY MS. CHEN:
 
Q Ms. Pig, how long have you lived in your
current residence?
 
A Four years.
 
Q And the construction material?
 
A Fired clay brick. Type N mortar. Standard
running bond pattern.
 
Q Why brick?
 
A Because I take my home seriously.
 
Q Your brothers arrived on March 14th?
 
A Yes. Marcus around midday. Then both of them
together about forty minutes later. Shaken.
 
Q And did the defendant come to your home?
 
A He did. Around seven in the evening.
 
Q What happened?
 
A He huffed. He puffed. He made quite a spectacle
of himself, frankly.
 
Q And did the structure hold?
 
A It held.
 
Q Through all of the defendant’s attempts?
 
A Every single one.
 
Q How many attempts did he make?
 
A Four that I counted. Possibly five. At one
point he climbed up onto the roof. Toward
the chimney.
 
Q What did you do?
 
A We lit the fire. He appeared to reconsider.
He came down. And he left.
 
Q And where are your brothers today, Ms. Pig?
 
A Safe. We are all safe.
 
MS. CHEN: No further questions, Your Honor.
 
THE COURT: Mr. Briggs, any cross?
 
MR. BRIGGS: No, Your Honor. The defense has no
questions for this witness.

(Defendant B.B. Wolf, sworn, takes the stand.)
 
DIRECT EXAMINATION BY MR. BRIGGS:
 
Q Mr. Wolf, did you intend to cause harm to
the plaintiffs on March 14th?
 
A Absolutely not.
 
Q Can you tell the court what happened?
 
A I was in the neighborhood. I have a medical
condition. A deviated septum. I sneeze
involuntarily and the force of it is —
admittedly substantial.
 
Q So you did not willfully destroy these
structures?
 
A It was a sneeze. An uncontrolled exhalation.
I am the victim here, in a sense. Of my own
sinuses.
 
MR. BRIGGS: Nothing further.
 
CROSS-EXAMINATION BY MS. CHEN:
 
Q Mr. Wolf, you have a deviated septum?
 
A That is correct.
 
Q And yet you have no medical records, no
physician visits, no diagnosis whatsoever
for this condition?
 
A I hadn’t gotten around to seeing a doctor.
 
Q You told each plaintiff, before each incident,
that you would “huff and puff” and destroy
their homes. Did you not?
 
A I may have said something to that effect.
 
Q That sounds like a plan, Mr. Wolf. Not a
sneeze. Did you blow down the first house?
 
A I — technically the aerodynamic pressure
from my exhalation —
 
Q A simple yes or no, Mr. Wolf.
 
A …Yes.
 
Q Did you blow down the second house?
 
A Yes.
 
Q Did you then climb onto Ms. Pig’s roof and
attempt entry through the chimney?
 
A I was looking for a way to — to seek shelter.
 
Q From what?
 
A I —
 
MS. CHEN: No further questions, Your Honor.

(All parties present. Jury absent — bench trial.)
 
THE COURT: Thank you. Counsel, I have considered
the evidence and all testimony presented
in this proceeding.
 
THE COURT: The defendant’s claim of involuntary
exhalation is, to put it charitably,
unpersuasive.
 
THE COURT: The testimony of all three witnesses
was consistent, credible, and mutually
corroborated. I found each of them to
be entirely forthright.
 
THE COURT: On Count One, Willful Destruction of
Property (Straw):
 
GUILTY.
 
THE COURT: On Count Two, Willful Destruction of
Property (Sticks):
 
GUILTY.
 
THE COURT: On Count Three, Attempted Destruction
of Property (Bricks):
 
GUILTY.
 
THE COURT: On Count Four, Unlawful Threatening
Behavior:
 
GUILTY.
 
THE COURT: As to sentencing. The court imposes
240 hours of community service, to be
served at the Northern Plains Housing
Authority, where you will build things,
Mr. Wolf, rather than destroy them.
 
THE COURT: Additionally, a restraining order is
hereby entered. The defendant will
refrain from approaching within 500 feet
of any swine-occupied residential structure.
 
THE COURT: Mr. Wolf, you are remanded to the
custody of the court officer pending
appeal review. This court is adjourned.
 
[ GAVEL ]
 
( END OF PROCEEDINGS )
